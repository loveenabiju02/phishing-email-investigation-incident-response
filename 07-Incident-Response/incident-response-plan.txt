# Incident Response Plan
## Incident ID: GH-2026-001
## Organisation: Gulf Horizon Travel LLC

### 1. Incident Overview
A simulated phishing email impersonating Microsoft 365 Support was delivered to an employee. The email used urgent account-verification language and directed the employee to a fraudulent login page.

The investigation simulation assumes that the employee accessed the link and submitted fictional test credentials. No real credentials were collected or transmitted.

### 2. Incident Classification
- Incident type: Phishing / Credential Exposure
- Severity: High
- Affected asset: Employee Microsoft 365 account (simulated)
- Status: Simulated incident under investigation
- Detection method: Email analysis and Splunk SIEM detection

### 3. Containment
Recommended immediate actions:
1. Temporarily restrict or secure the potentially affected account.
2. Reset the employee's password.
3. Revoke active sessions and refresh tokens.
4. Enforce or verify multi-factor authentication.
5. Block the confirmed malicious URL and associated domain through appropriate security controls.
6. Search email logs for other recipients of the same message.

### 4. Eradication
1. Remove the phishing email from affected mailboxes.
2. Identify and remove any related malicious inbox rules or forwarding rules.
3. Check endpoints for suspicious downloads or execution.
4. Review account activity for unauthorised changes.
5. Remove any confirmed malicious artefacts.

### 5. Recovery
1. Restore normal account access after securing the account.
2. Verify legitimate sign-ins and account configuration.
3. Monitor authentication activity for suspicious behaviour.
4. Confirm that affected users can access services securely.
5. Retain relevant evidence for future investigation.

### 6. Lessons Learned
- Employees should verify unexpected account-security requests through official channels.
- Urgent messages threatening account suspension should be treated cautiously.
- Email authentication controls such as SPF, DKIM and DMARC should be monitored.
- Multi-factor authentication and session revocation are important account-protection measures.
- Security awareness training should include phishing links and credential-harvesting scenarios.
- SIEM monitoring can help identify suspicious access and credential-submission activity.

### 7. Evidence and Limitations
Evidence includes the synthetic phishing email, SHA-256 file hash, extracted indicators, forensic notes, Python analysis output, simulated web activity CSV, Splunk searches, dashboard and alert configuration.

This is a controlled portfolio simulation. No real employee account, endpoint, email gateway or Microsoft 365 tenant was compromised or investigated. Containment, eradication and recovery actions are recommendations, not actions performed on a live environment.

### 8. Incident Closure Criteria
The simulated incident may be considered resolved when:
- The affected account is secured.
- Suspicious access has been reviewed.
- Malicious email indicators have been blocked where verified.
- Evidence is preserved.
- Lessons learned and recommendations are documented.
